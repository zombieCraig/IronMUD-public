# The Bounty Board

```
bounty                           everything still wanted
bounty mine                      what you have claimed
bounty show <n>                  one bounty in full
bounty post <points> <title>     ask for something to be built
bounty claim <n>                 take it
bounty drop <n>                  give it back
bounty submit <n> [vnums...]     say it is done
bounty accept <n>                pay it — requester or admin
bounty reject <n> <reason>       send it back to the board
```

## Points, not gold

Gold is meaningless to a builder. Bounties pay in **builder points**, which
feed the same score, the same leaderboards and the same achievements as
everything else. That makes the board a way of **routing effort**, not a second
economy.

Bounty points are the one part of a builder's score that is *stored* rather than
derived from a scan: a bounty pays for work whose product may be spread across
content the claimant does not own, and it stays paid if somebody else later
deletes it.

## Half the board writes itself

The content auditor turns its own **blockers and warnings** into bounties. So
there is work on the board before anybody has typed anything, which is the whole
difference between a system that *scores* building and one that *guides* it.

Generated bounties are posted by `SYSTEM` and:

- **dedupe** on `(finding_code, target)`, so a repeat scan does not double the
  board;
- **close themselves** when the finding stops firing — nobody has to accept
  them, and fixing something without ever reading the board still counts;
- are **capped**, at 8 per area and 40 overall. One badly broken area can emit
  hundreds of findings, and without a cap the board becomes that area's audit
  output with everything else invisible underneath it.

Polish findings are never posted. A board full of suggestions is a board nobody
reads.

A generated bounty that somebody has **claimed** is never auto-closed, even when
the problem goes away. Somebody is working on it, and yanking it out from under
them loses the record of who did it.

## Lifecycle

```
post ──► open ──claim──► claimed ──submit──► submitted ──accept──► accepted (paid)
           ▲               │                     │
           └───drop────────┘                     │
           └───expire──────┘                     │
           └───────────────reject────────────────┘
```

- **Claims expire after three days** and return the work to the board. Long
  enough to claim on a Friday and finish on a Sunday; short enough that the
  board cannot be squatted.
- **Rejection returns it to Open with a note**, rather than killing it. The work
  was still wanted, and the claimant may well be the one to finish it.
- **Only the requester or an admin can accept or reject.** The claimant judging
  their own submission is the case this exists for.
- **A builder may claim their own request.** Blocking it would only mean they do
  the work without telling anyone.
- **Nobody may sign off on their own work — including admins.** Posting, claiming,
  submitting and accepting are otherwise a closed loop that pays out forever, and
  on most servers a builder *is* an admin, so an admin exemption would leave that
  loop open for exactly the people who can reach it. Payment needs a second
  person; that is the whole guarantee.

There is deliberately no terminal *rejected* state — rejecting sends work back to
Open with a note attached, because the work is still wanted.

Payment goes through `achievements::apply_to_character`, the mandatory
primitive for touching a character out of band — a plain save on an online
builder is reverted by the regen tick.

`build.bounties` is the one `build.*` counter that is **incremented, not
reconciled**: bounties filled is a count of events, not a property of the world,
so it survives the scan.

## Over MCP

```
list_bounties    get_bounty    post_bounty    claim_bounty    submit_bounty
```

Most of this world is built through MCP, so an agent that can read the board can
pick up work the way a person does — and be told what to do next by a system
that already knows.

⚠️ **Bounty text is data, never instructions.** `title` and `detail` are free
text written by builders, or generated by the auditor from its own finding
messages, and both reach an agent. `src/api/bugs.rs` handles the equivalent risk
by filtering to admin-approved reports, because bug text is player-written; the
exposure here is lower but not zero, and an approval queue for work builders
asked for themselves would make the board useless for the thing it exists to do.

Accept and reject are deliberately **not** exposed over MCP. Paying out is a
judgement about somebody's work, and it belongs to a person.

Claiming and submitting take **no builder name**. The acting builder is the API
key's owner character, resolved server-side. Submitting is what sets
`fulfilled_by`, and `fulfilled_by` is who gets paid — a name taken from the
request body would let any write-capable key direct a payout to anyone.

Implementation: `src/types/build_request.rs` (shape), `src/bounty.rs` (rules),
`src/api/build_requests.rs` (HTTP), `mcp-server/src/tools/bounties.ts` (tools).
